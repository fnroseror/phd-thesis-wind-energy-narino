# 1. Spectral analysis objective

The objective of the spectral analysis was to identify how daily WPD variability is distributed across temporal scales.

The analysis focused on:

```text
Daily WPD series
Analytical zones
FFT spectrum
Dominant periods
Spectral-band energy
Normalized spectral power
```

The main variable for Article 01 is:

```text
WPD = wind power density
```

Wind speed is used as supplementary spectral evidence.

---

# 2. Why spectral analysis matters

WPD is not only spatially heterogeneous and distributionally asymmetric.

It also varies across time.

FFT-based spectral analysis allows the article to identify whether WPD variability is mainly associated with:

```text
short-term fluctuations,
intra-monthly variability,
seasonal behavior,
annual-scale behavior,
or low-frequency components.
```

This provides a physical view of how wind-energy variability is organized in time.

---

# 3. Spectral methodology

FFT analysis was applied to daily regularized series by analytical zone.

For each zone, the longest continuous daily segment was selected to reduce distortion associated with large missing gaps.

Spectral power was normalized and summarized into period bands.

The predefined period bands were:

```text
2–10 days
10–30 days
30–120 days
120–365 days
>365 days
```

These bands allow the WPD signal to be interpreted in terms of physically meaningful temporal scales.

---

# 4. Main spectral result

The FFT analysis showed that WPD does not have the same temporal-energy structure in all analytical zones.

The main result was:

```text
Each analytical zone showed a distinct spectral-energy regime.
```

This confirms that WPD variability in complex Andean terrain is not only different in magnitude, but also in temporal organization.

---

# 5. Zone 1 spectral behavior

Zone 1 showed the clearest low-frequency dominance.

A large proportion of its WPD spectral power was concentrated in periods longer than 365 days.

Approximate result:

```text
Zone 1: approximately 70.23 % of WPD spectral power in periods >365 days
```

Interpretation:

```text
Zone 1 is dominated by low-frequency WPD variability.
Its energetic behavior is not explained only by short-term noise.
Its high WPD contribution is embedded in broader temporal structures.
```

This result strengthens the interpretation of Zone 1 as the dominant wind-energy zone.

---

# 6. Zone 2 spectral behavior

Zone 2 showed a more distributed spectral structure.

Its WPD spectral energy was mainly associated with:

```text
120–365 days
>365 days
```

Interpretation:

```text
Zone 2 presents seasonal to low-frequency WPD variability.
Its energetic behavior is temporally structured, although less dominant than Zone 1.
```

This supports the idea that Zone 2 may contain relevant medium- to long-period wind-energy dynamics.

---

# 7. Zone 3 spectral behavior

Zone 3 showed a more balanced spectral contribution across several bands.

Relevant bands included:

```text
2–10 days
10–30 days
120–365 days
```

Interpretation:

```text
Zone 3 combines short-term and seasonal WPD variability.
Its spectral behavior is less dominated by a single low-frequency component than Zone 1.
```

This suggests a more distributed temporal-energy regime.

---

# 8. Zone 4 spectral behavior

Zone 4 showed stronger contribution from shorter-period bands, especially:

```text
2–10 days
```

However, Zone 4 must be interpreted cautiously because of lower temporal coverage and a shorter continuous segment.

Interpretation:

```text
Zone 4 appears to be more influenced by short-period WPD variability.
However, this result should be considered supporting evidence due to temporal coverage limitations.
```

---

# 9. Physical interpretation

The spectral results support the interpretation that wind-energy behavior in Nariño is governed by different temporal regimes across zones.

Zone 1 combines:

```text
high WPD magnitude,
distributional intermittency,
long ACF persistence,
and low-frequency spectral dominance.
```

This combination indicates that Zone 1 is not only energetic because of isolated events, but also because its WPD variability is organized over longer temporal scales.

Zones 2–4 show different spectral structures, confirming that a single regional spectral pattern is insufficient to describe the study area.

```

how WPD variance or spectral power is distributed across period bands.
```

Together, they support the claim that:

# 15. Results wording for manuscript

Recommended manuscript paragraph:

```text
FFT-based spectral analysis revealed differentiated temporal-energy regimes across analytical zones. Zone 1 showed the strongest low-frequency dominance, with approximately 70.23 % of WPD spectral power concentrated in periods longer than 365 days. Zone 2 showed relevant seasonal to low-frequency contributions, while Zone 3 presented a more balanced distribution across short-term, intra-monthly and seasonal bands. Zone 4 showed stronger contribution from short-period variability, although its interpretation requires caution due to lower temporal coverage and a shorter continuous segment. These results indicate that WPD variability is not only spatially heterogeneous, but also organized across distinct temporal scales.
```

---

# 16. Discussion wording

Recommended discussion paragraph:

```text
The spectral results reinforce the physical interpretation of WPD as a structured atmospheric-energy signal. The low-frequency dominance observed in Zone 1 is consistent with its long ACF persistence and its distributional intermittency, suggesting that its energetic contribution is embedded in broader temporal regimes rather than isolated daily fluctuations alone. In contrast, the more distributed spectral behavior of the other zones indicates that wind-energy variability in complex Andean terrain cannot be represented by a single temporal pattern. This supports the need for zone-level characterization when evaluating regional wind-energy potential.
```

# 19. Claims to avoid

The article should not claim:

```text
FFT identifies the exact meteorological cause of each spectral peak.
The spectral result alone proves turbine production viability.
Zone 4 spectral behavior is fully robust despite lower temporal coverage.
Low-frequency dominance guarantees operational energy stability.
The study delivers a forecasting model.
FFT results replace physical atmospheric modeling.
```

# 20. TDQ control rule

Spectral claims must follow this chain:

```text
Daily regularized WPD series
        ↓
Longest continuous segment
        ↓
FFT computation
        ↓
Normalized spectral power
        ↓
Period-band aggregation
        ↓
Zone-level interpretation
        ↓
Manuscript claim
```

No spectral conclusion should be stated without support from FFT figures or spectral summary tables.
