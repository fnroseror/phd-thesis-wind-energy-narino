Figure_01
Figure 1. Evolution of installed wind-power capacity at global, South American, and Colombian scales from 2016 to 2025. Annual observations are shown as points connected by line segments to emphasize both the discrete yearly records and the temporal trajectory. Panels use independent vertical scales to preserve readability. Capacity corresponds to maximum net generating capacity installed and connected at the end of each calendar year. Source: IRENA (2026), Renewable Capacity Statistics 2026.
Figure_02
Figure 2. Physiographic setting, IDEAM meteorological stations, and analytical aggregation units in Nariño, Colombia. The four analytical units organize the observed monitoring network for comparative analysis and should not be interpreted as homogeneous wind-resource regions or continuous spatial resource estimates.
Figure_03
Figure 3. Revised methodological framework for the physical-statistical characterization of wind power density. Quality control and nonlinear energetic transformation are applied at station-hour scale before equal-station zonal aggregation. Subsequent analyses quantify distributional structure, station-composition sensitivity, temporal dependence, spectral organization, and uncertainty.
Figure_04
Figure 4. Monthly mean wind speed and wind power density for the four analytical units using the canonical V2 dataset. Wind speed and WPD are shown separately because local station-hour WPD is calculated before equal-station zonal aggregation.
Figure_05
Figure 5. Empirical cumulative wind-speed distributions with fitted Weibull and Rayleigh models by analytical unit. Solid black lines show the empirical CDF, dashed burgundy lines show Weibull fits, and dotted gray lines show Rayleigh fits. Weibull provides a clear relative improvement over Rayleigh in Zone 1, whereas model performance in Zones 2–4 depends on the selected goodness-of-fit metric.
Figure_06
Figure 6. Direct contribution of individual IDEAM stations to the long-term zonal mean WPD. Contributions sum to 100% within each analytical unit and quantify the dependence of the aggregated energetic signal on the composition of the observed station network.
Figure_07
Figure 7. Sensitivity of calendar-preserving WPD lag correlations to short-gap interpolation. Solid black lines use observed calendar pairs; blue dashed lines use linear interpolation only for internal gaps of seven days or less. Their close agreement indicates that short-lag persistence is not generated by the interpolation procedure.
Figure_08
Figure 8. Fraction of one-sided WPD spectral power by period band for the longest continuous daily segment available in each analytical unit after interpolation of internal gaps of seven days or less. Spectral fractions are interpreted as descriptors of temporal variability rather than as evidence of deterministic atmospheric cycles.
Figure_S1
Figure S1. Empirical wind-speed density with fitted Weibull and Rayleigh models by analytical unit. Solid black lines show empirical densities, dashed burgundy lines show Weibull fits, and dotted gray lines show Rayleigh fits. The figure is retained to distinguish relative model performance from absolute goodness of fit, particularly in Zone 1, where Weibull improves substantially on Rayleigh but does not reproduce the empirical density perfectly near the lowest wind-speed states.
