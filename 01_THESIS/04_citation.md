## What to cite

Cite the thesis when referring to:

- the scientific argument;
- methodology and objectives;
- approved figures, tables, and results;
- conclusions and doctoral contributions;
- interpretation of WPD, TDQ-PIESS/KFAS, `I_TDQ`, FNRR, `E_free`, and `E_usable`.

Cite the repository when referring to:

- code;
- data metadata;
- canonical output files;
- digital appendices;
- manifests, hashes, and validation logs;
- computational reproducibility support.

When both the scientific result and its computational object are used, cite both the thesis and repository.

## Version-sensitive use

Because the repository may receive non-scientific maintenance updates, reproducible work should cite a release tag or commit identifier once the final public version is frozen. The commit or release must be recorded together with the validation log.
